When a clogged drain is an emergency

Call right away if water is rising in a tub or shower when you flush, if a toilet and a sink back up together, or if a floor drain is bubbling. Those signs usually point to the main line instead of one fixture, and the water has nowhere else to go but into the house.

Our line is staffed 24/7, so nights, weekends and holidays are normal hours for us. Here is what to do before the plumber arrives:

  1. Stop using every drain in the house, including washing machines and dishwashers.
  2. Skip the store-bought chemical openers. They sit in a stopped pipe and can burn the next person who works on it.
  3. Move rugs, boxes and electronics away from the lowest drain, which is usually the tub or a floor drain.
  4. Find your main water shut-off in case a supply line is involved, and keep the area around it clear.
  5. Call (919) 944-7281 and tell us which fixtures back up, so the right equipment is on the truck.

Why drains clog in Knightdale homes

Knightdale has two very different housing ages, and both put pressure on drain lines. Near the old downtown, many houses were built for railroad families after the Norfolk and Southern line arrived in the early 1900s, according to the Town of Knightdale's own history page. South of US 64, Parkside, Planter's Walk and the Mingo Creek subdivision grew after the Mingo Creek sewer outfall opened in the late 1980s.

Those neighborhoods are no longer new. A drain line of any material collects scale, soap film and grease over thirty years, and a bend that never gave trouble in 1995 can trap a clump of wipes today. That is why we look at the pipe, not just the clog.

Raleigh Water, which serves Knightdale, names grease and so-called flushable wipes as two of the main causes of sewer backups. Its guidance for homes is to wipe grease from pans with a paper towel and to keep wipes out of the toilet, because a garbage disposal is not designed to handle fats, oils and grease.

How we clear and prove a drain is open

We do not hand you a clear sink and leave. Each visit ends with a flow test, and when the history points to a bigger problem we show you why.

Cable machines

Most household clogs are cleared with an electric drain cable fitted with a blade or retriever head. The cable cuts through soft blockages and hooks hair, wipes and small objects.

Hydro jetting when cabling is not enough

A cable opens a hole through grease. A water jet scours the pipe wall. If the same drain has stopped twice in a season, we explain the difference and recommend jetting.

Camera check on repeat clogs

When a line keeps clogging, a drain camera shows whether the cause is buildup, a sag, a cracked section or roots. Findings feed into sewer inspection or repair advice, with no pressure to buy anything on the spot.

Kitchen sink clogs and the dishwasher connection

Kitchen lines clog more often than bathroom lines because grease cools and sticks as it travels. The garbage disposal does not break grease down, it only chops food so it can travel further before it lodges. If your disposal hums and the sink fills, a jam or a clog may be involved, and garbage disposal repair is a separate fix from clearing the line.

Dishwasher drain hoses also join the same trap, so a dishwasher that leaves standing water often means the sink branch is partly blocked. We check both before reassembling.

Who owns the clog: your pipe or the city's

In Knightdale, water and sewer service is handled by the City of Raleigh. Raleigh's own guidance says the property owner is responsible for the sewer line from the house to the city main, and the city handles the main in the street. Most backups happen on the private side.

That matters when you hire a plumber. Raleigh recommends asking the plumber to clear the blockage all the way to the city main. We do that, and if the blockage is in the street we tell you so you can report it to Raleigh Water. Our sewer line cleaning page covers main line work in more detail.

A slow drain that also leaves a damp spot nearby may be a leaking drain line instead of a clog. If that is the case, you need leak detection rather than more snaking.

Traps, vents and cleanouts are covered by the North Carolina State Building Code: Plumbing Code, the state's edition of the International Plumbing Code (IPC). If a visit shows a missing cleanout or a badly set trap, we tell you what compliant looks like.

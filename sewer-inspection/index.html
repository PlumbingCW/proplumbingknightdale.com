What a sewer inspection shows

A small waterproof camera on a flexible cable travels from the cleanout toward the city connection. It records the pipe material, joints, offsets, sags, cracks, roots and buildup, and it can measure the distance to each problem so a repair can be planned precisely.

Without a camera, a repair is a guess. With one, the crew digs in the right place and you know what you are paying for.

Buying a home in Knightdale

A standard home inspection does not scope the sewer lateral. That is a gap for older houses, especially the railroad-era homes near downtown Knightdale, where clay or cast iron pipe and mature trees are common. A camera report is cheap relative to a surprise replacement after you move in.

For newer homes in subdivisions such as Parkside and Planter's Walk, the usual findings are sags and joint offsets rather than corrosion. Either way, you get a recording to share with your agent or to use in price discussions.

No pressure

What affects inspection pricing

We quote a clear price before work starts. The factors are:

Access.

A cleanout makes the inspection simple. Without one, we may need another access point.

Line length.

Longer runs take longer to scope.

Cleaning first.

A line packed with grease must be cleared before the camera can pass.

Report needs.

A recording only is simpler than a written summary for a sale.

Timing.

After-hours visits are priced differently than scheduled ones.

Insurance note: an inspection is usually not covered, but the report can support a claim after a sudden backup or collapse. Ask your insurer what documentation they need.

The city main and your lateral

Raleigh Water serves Knightdale and maintains the main in the street. The property owner is responsible for the line from the house to that main. If the camera finds the problem is in the city's section, we show you where, and you can report it to Raleigh Water.

If the line turns out to be only grease-lined, hydro jetting is often the next step.

What counts as a defect, such as a bellied section or a missing cleanout, is judged against the North Carolina State Building Code: Plumbing Code, the state's edition of the International Plumbing Code (IPC), which is the standard we explain your findings in.

Knightdale: our home town

Knightdale was shaped by the railroad, and the older homes near the downtown rail line were built for railroad workers. The Mingo Creek sewer outfall opened in the late 1980s and made room for subdivisions such as Parkside, Planter's Walk and Mingo Creek south of US 64.

That gives Knightdale two housing eras side by side. Older houses call for sewer and water line attention, and subdivisions from the late 1980s onward are reaching the age when water heaters, fixtures and supply lines need replacing. Our primary ZIP here is 27545.

Knightdale's major roads are US 64, US 64 Business, which is Knightdale Boulevard, and I-540, which is how our trucks reach the whole area quickly. For anything urgent, call (919) 944-7281.

How emergency dispatch works

Our phone line is staffed around the clock. When you call, tell us your address, what is happening and whether water is still running. We give guidance on shutting things off and send the closest available plumber.

Response time depends on traffic and where crews are working, so we give you an honest estimate on the call rather than a promised arrival time. Every address we serve gets the same clear price before work starts.

All three towns, same standards

Water and sewer service for all four towns is provided by Raleigh Water, so the question of who owns which pipe is the same everywhere. The city handles the main. The owner handles the line from the meter and the sewer lateral to the main.

Browse our plumbing services to see what we do, or go straight to emergency water heater replacement or drain cleaning.

What to do before we arrive, wherever you are

If water is flowing, close the main valve, switch off power to wet areas and move valuables away from the lowest point in the house. If sewage is backing up, stop using every drain, and do not flush.

Then call (919) 944-7281. Tell us your town, your street or neighborhood and what you see, and we will start dispatch while you stay on the line. If you smell gas near a water heater or furnace, leave the house and call 911 or the gas utility first.

What changes from town to town

The plumbing itself is the same in every town, but the problems that show up first follow the housing. Wendell mixes a 1903 downtown with the Wendell Falls planned community, so old drain lines and young water heaters both appear. Zebulon is a 1907 railroad town where aging laterals and supply lines lead the list.

Rolesville was incorporated in 1837 but most of it has been built since 2000, so first-generation water heaters and builder-grade fixtures are the common calls. Knightdale sits in the middle with both eras. We staff the phone with that in mind, and we ask the age of your house first, because that single answer tells us more about the likely problem than almost anything else you can say on the phone.

Where Knightdale Station sits

Knightdale Station Park is at 810 N. First Avenue. The Town of Knightdale describes it as a 71-acre park with an amphitheater, athletic fields, a dog park, a splash pad, an inclusive playground and paved trails. The Mingo Creek Greenway connects to the park.

The residential community of the same name sits next to the park. The Town's development project list shows Knightdale Station (case S-3-13) as a 611-lot subdivision under construction, with inspections ongoing. That tells us the neighborhood is still being built out, so some streets have mostly new homes and others are still filling in.

A separate planned development, Downtown North at Knightdale Station, was proposed in a 2022 sketch plan for about 91 acres with townhomes, condos, single-family lots and commercial space. We treat it as a plan, not a finished place.

Plumbing in a newer community

A local real-estate guide describes most Knightdale Station homes as built within the last 10 to 15 years, in single-family plans of roughly 2,400 to 3,300 square feet. Treat that as a general guide only, because lots are still being built and sold.

We do not claim that newer homes have particular plumbing defects. What we can say is general: a home that is only a few years old may still be inside a builder or manufacturer warranty, and that can change who you should call first.

Check warranty paperwork first

If a water heater, faucet or fixture fails early, look for the builder warranty and the manufacturer's paperwork before you pay for a repair. Take photos of the problem and the data label on the equipment.

Water heaters and tankless units

Tank and tankless water heaters both need basic upkeep. Our water heater repair and tankless water heater pages explain what we look at and when a flush or a repair makes sense.

Know your shut-off valves

Find the main shut-off and the shut-offs under each sink and behind each toilet before you need them. Turn each one gently once a year so it does not seize.

Before you call about a leak

These steps limit damage while you wait for help.

  • Close the nearest shut-off valve, or the main valve if you cannot find the local one.
  • Turn off power to a wet area only if you can reach the breaker without standing in water.
  • Move rugs and electronics away from the water and take photos for your records.
  • Do not use chemical drain cleaners on a backup, because they can harm pipes and anyone who works on the drain later.
Local note

Who owns which pipe

Raleigh Water provides water and sewer service in Knightdale. As a general rule, the city maintains the main and the meter, and the property owner maintains the line from the meter to the house and the sewer lateral from the house to the city main. Ask Raleigh Water to confirm where your responsibility starts. Plumbing work here follows the North Carolina State Building Code: Plumbing Code.

New work may need a permit and inspection. The Town of Knightdale's Development Services department handles development approvals, and Wake County Permits and Inspections handles plumbing inspections.

Sources: Town of Knightdale park and project list pages, Raleigh Water, Wake County.

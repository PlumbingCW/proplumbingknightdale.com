What we know about Widewaters

Widewaters is a named Knightdale community with a clubhouse, pool, walking trails and a tot lot according to local real-estate guides. They place it near I-540 and the Widewaters Commons Shopping Center, and describe 3 to 5 bedroom homes of roughly 1,300 to 3,200 square feet with a homeowners association.

Sources disagree on timing. One guide says the master-planned community was established in 2004, while another says most homes were built after 2010. Both could be true if the community was built in phases, so we do not give a single build year.

Larger households and hot water

We do not claim that Widewaters homes have a particular plumbing defect. The practical point is general: homes with three to five bedrooms often have more people using hot water at the same time.

When a tank water heater runs out of hot water too soon, the cause may be the heater's size, a failed element or burner, or sediment. A plumber can tell which by checking the unit.

Replacement or repair

If your tank is leaking at the base, it is usually at the end of its life. See water heater replacement and water heater repair to understand the difference.

Considering tankless

A tankless unit can suit a busy household, but it changes gas, venting and electrical needs. Our tankless water heater page explains what to expect.

Slow drains in bathrooms

If several bathroom drains slow down together, the cause may be a shared line rather than one fixture. See drain cleaning.

Local note

Raleigh Water and the pipes you own

Raleigh Water provides water and sewer service in Knightdale. As a general rule, the city maintains the main and the meter, and the property owner maintains the line from the meter to the house and the sewer lateral from the house to the city main. Ask Raleigh Water to confirm where your responsibility starts. Plumbing work here follows the North Carolina State Building Code: Plumbing Code.

Water heater work in North Carolina is licensed plumbing work, and some jobs need a permit. A plumber can tell you whether yours does.

Sources: Raleigh Water, North Carolina Board of Examiners of Plumbing, Heating and Fire Sprinkler Contractors.

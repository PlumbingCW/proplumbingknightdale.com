Two Wendells: the 1903 core and Wendell Falls

Wendell was incorporated in 1903, and the town has two National Register historic districts, the Wendell Boulevard Historic District and the Wendell Commercial Historic District. Houses of that era have had a century of repairs, additions and changing codes behind their walls.

Wendell Falls is the opposite case. It is one of the largest planned communities in North Carolina, and its homes are far newer. Different ages fail in different ways, so we ask the age of the house first and plan the visit around the answer.

Before we arrive in Wendell

Water in the wrong place is an emergency at any hour. Do these four things while a plumber is on the way.

1Shut off the water.

Turn the main valve clockwise. If it is in a crawlspace or garage, flip a flashlight on first.

2Kill power near the water.

Switch off breakers for wet rooms. Do not step into standing water near outlets.

3Clear the area.

Move rugs, boxes and electronics away from the leak and from the lowest drain.

Local note

Water service for Wendell customers

The Town of Wendell's own public works page says water and sewer in town are handled by the City of Raleigh, and it lists Raleigh Water's customer line, (919) 996-3245, for leaks, billing and new accounts. That matters when you are deciding who to call first.

If you see water coming up near the meter or in the street, call Raleigh Water. If the leak is on your side, between the meter and the house or inside it, call us. We can tell you which it is, and our water line repair page explains how that boundary works.

The town's page also notes that some owners rely on private wells and points them to Wake County for water testing. Well systems have pressure tanks and pumps that differ from a city connection, so tell us which you have when you call.

Work in Wendell follows the North Carolina State Building Code: Plumbing Code, the state's edition of the International Plumbing Code (IPC), whatever the age of the house.

The rail line and the ground under Wendell

A freight line, the Carolina Coastal Railway, still runs through downtown, and passenger trains stopped in Wendell until 1943. Wendell also lies where the Piedmont meets the Atlantic Coastal Plain, a transition that can mean different soils from one end of town to the other.

The practical point: buried lines move with the ground. A water or sewer lateral that has never been a problem can start leaking at a joint after years of wet and dry cycles. A camera from our sewer inspection service shows what is happening without guessing.

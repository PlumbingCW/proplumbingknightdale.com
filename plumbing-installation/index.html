Permits and inspections in Knightdale

Whether your project needs a permit depends on its scope. The Town of Knightdale Development Services is the place to ask, and plumbing inspections in town are carried out through Wake County Permits and Inspections. Adding fixtures, moving drains or altering supply lines commonly triggers a permit.

Hire a licensed plumbing contractor. The State Board of Examiners of Plumbing, Heating and Fire Sprinkler Contractors publishes a public license search at public.nclicensing.org. We tell you which steps apply to your project before work starts.

Slab, crawlspace and what it means for new lines

Many Knightdale homes sit on a crawlspace or a slab, and the difference changes everything about new plumbing. A crawlspace gives easy access for new lines. A slab means cutting concrete or routing through walls and attics.

Clay-rich Piedmont soil also affects underground lines and sump placement. We plan the route and the discharge point so water goes where it should.

What decides the cost of an installation

We quote a clear price before work starts. The usual variables are:

Number and type of fixtures.

A single vanity is a smaller job than a whole bathroom.

Access.

Slab, finished walls and second floors add labor.

Permits and inspection.

Larger jobs need scheduling around inspections.

Fixtures supplied.

Customer-supplied fixtures differ from ones we source.

Rough-in changes.

Moving drains or vents costs more than keeping them in place.

Insurance note: planned installs are normally out of pocket. If the project follows a covered loss, such as a burst pipe, ask your insurer which parts it will pay for.

What to do before we arrive

Clear a path to the work area, and know where your main shut-off valve is. Our plumber will want to close the water at the start and test it again at the end.

Do not cut into a wall or slab yourself. Pipes, wires and gas lines can share a cavity, and a wrong cut is costly. Tell us if the house has a history of leaks or if anyone has changed the plumbing before.

Water quality and fixtures

Raleigh Water's November 2025 finished water report shows total hardness of 23.9 mg/L as calcium carbonate at the plant tap. Soft water means fixtures and heaters see slower mineral buildup than in hard-water towns, which is one less thing to design around.

If a fixture is failing rather than being replaced for style, see faucet repair first.

New supply, drain and vent work follows the North Carolina State Building Code: Plumbing Code, the state's edition of the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and rough-in is inspected before walls close where a permit applies.

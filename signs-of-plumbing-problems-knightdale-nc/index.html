Signs of Plumbing Problems: A Knightdale, NC Homeowner Guide

.

Most serious plumbing failures send out warning signs weeks or months before they become emergencies. Slow drains, a bill that creeps up, damp patches, new noises and low pressure are the common ones. Catching them early is almost always cheaper than waiting.

This guide groups the warning signs by what they usually mean and tells you what to do first.

Warning signs and what they usually mean

Any one sign can have a harmless cause. Several together are worth a call.

Slow drains in several fixtures.Main line buildup or a vent problem.Check which fixtures are affected.
Gurgling or bubbling.Air trapped in the drain system.Note when it happens.

Drains and sewer lines

A single slow sink is usually a local clog. When the tub, toilet and kitchen all slow together, the main line is the likely cause. Sewer smells, damp patches in the yard and lush green strips of lawn over the line can all signal a leaking or broken sewer.

The earlier you catch a main line problem, the more options you have. See our sewer inspection page for how a camera helps, and drain cleaning for routine clearing.

Leaks you cannot see

Hidden leaks show up on the bill first. Check the water meter with every tap and appliance off. If the leak indicator or dial moves, water is escaping. Warm spots on a floor over a slab, the sound of running water or mildew smells add to the evidence.

Our leak detection page describes how we find a leak without opening walls at random.

Do a ten-minute plumbing check twice a year

It catches most early problems.

  • Look under every sink and around every toilet for damp spots.
  • Read the water meter before and after an hour of no water use.
  • Inspect the water heater for rust, leaks and the age on its label.
  • Test the main shut-off and the angle stops.
  • Check exposed pipes in the crawlspace, attic and garage.
Knightdale note

What aging housing means for Knightdale plumbing

The Town of Knightdale's own history describes older homes near the downtown rail line, built for railroad families. Houses in subdivisions south of US 64, such as Parkside, Planter's Walk and Mingo Creek, grew after the Mingo Creek sewer outfall opened in the late 1980s, though build years vary by section, so check the age of your own fixtures.

Raleigh Water, which serves Knightdale, reported total hardness of 23.9 mg/L as calcium carbonate in its November 2025 finished water report, which is soft. That slows scale, but it does not stop corrosion in old metal pipe or wear in fixtures. Age is the better guide to what to check.

Most of what we check, from traps and vents to relief valves, is set out in the North Carolina State Building Code: Plumbing Code, the state's edition of the International Plumbing Code (IPC).

Sources: Town of Knightdale history page, Raleigh Water finished water report, November 2025.

Water heaters

A water heater that rumbles, leaks around the base, delivers less hot water than it used to or is over ten years old is one to watch. The earlier you plan a replacement, the more choices you have and the less likely it is to fail on a holiday night.

See water heater repair for fixes and water heater replacement for planning.

Fixtures and small leaks

Drips, running toilets and sweating shut-off valves look minor, but they waste water and often point to a part that is about to fail. A repair now is cheap. A part that fails during the night is not.

If you have several of these, ask us to look at all of them in one visit. Our faucet repair and toilet repair pages describe what we check.

Pressure problems

Water pressure that has dropped across the whole house usually points to a leak, a partly closed valve, a failing pressure regulator or a clogged service line. Pressure that is low at one fixture is more often a clogged aerator or a failing cartridge.

Very high pressure is a problem too. It strains pipes, fixtures and appliance hoses, and it can cause a pattern of leaks at joints. If you have repeated leaks, a pressure test is a cheap diagnostic step.

Noises tell a story

A banging pipe when a tap closes is water hammer, caused by a sudden stop in flow. It can loosen pipe supports and joints over time, and a plumber can add an arrestor or secure the pipe. A whistling tap often points to a worn washer or a partly closed valve.

A hissing sound when nothing is running deserves attention. It can be a running toilet or a small leak under pressure, and finding it early beats finding it in the ceiling.

Keep a plumbing log

Write down when a symptom started, how often it happens and what you tried. A log helps a plumber narrow the problem quickly, and it helps your insurer if a claim comes later. Photos with dates add to it.

Include the age of your water heater, any recent plumbing work and the location of shut-off valves. It takes five minutes and pays back the first time you need it.

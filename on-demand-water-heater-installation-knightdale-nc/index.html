On-Demand Water Heater Installation for Knightdale, NC Homes

.

An on-demand, or tankless, water heater heats water only when a tap is open, so it does not store a tank of hot water and does not run out during a long shower. The trade-off is installation: a tankless unit often needs a bigger gas line or a stronger electrical circuit than the tank it replaces.

Here is what the install involves, which homes are good candidates and how to decide between tankless and a tank in Knightdale.

How on-demand heating works

When you open a hot tap, a flow sensor detects the water moving and starts the burner or the electric elements. Water passes through a heat exchanger and leaves hot. When you close the tap, the unit shuts off.

Because water is heated as it flows, the unit has to supply the temperature rise you want at the flow rate you use. A bigger household with two showers running needs a larger unit than a couple with one bathroom.

Gas versus electric tankless

Fuel is the biggest installation variable.

Type.What the house needs.Watch out for.
Gas tankless.A gas line sized for the burner, a vent path and a condensate drain on high-efficiency models.Undersized gas pipe is the most common upgrade.
Electric whole-house.A large dedicated circuit, often more than an older panel can spare.Panel capacity and wiring cost.
Electric point-of-use.A smaller circuit near a single sink or shower.Limited flow and a low temperature rise.

What a proper tankless install includes

A unit on the wall is only part of the job.

  • Sizing by flow rate and temperature rise, not guesswork.
  • A gas line or circuit that matches the unit's rating.
  • Venting installed to the manufacturer's instructions.
  • Isolation valves with service ports so the unit can be flushed.
  • A condensate drain on condensing models and a pressure relief setup that meets code.
  • Removal and haul-away of the old tank.

Which Knightdale homes suit tankless

Homes in subdivisions south of US 64, such as Parkside and Planter's Walk, grew after the Mingo Creek sewer outfall opened in the late 1980s. Many have a tank heater in a garage or closet that is now at or past its normal life. That makes them a natural moment to consider a switch, as long as the gas line and venting can support it.

Older houses near the railroad-era downtown can also take a tankless unit, but the work is often larger because of older piping, tight closets and venting routes. A site visit answers it better than a guess.

Knightdale note

Soft water and tankless heaters

Raleigh Water, which serves Knightdale, reported total hardness of 23.9 mg/L as calcium carbonate at the plant tap in its November 2025 finished water report. That is soft water. Tankless units are the type most sensitive to mineral scale, so soft water is good news and means flushing can be less frequent than in hard-water towns.

It does not remove the need to flush. Follow the interval in the manual, and make sure the install includes service ports. Our tankless water heater page explains the maintenance.

Gas piping, venting and the relief setup on a tankless install follow the North Carolina State Building Code: Plumbing Code, the state's edition of the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and electric units follow the National Electrical Code (NEC) for the circuit.

Source: Raleigh Water finished water report, November 2025.

Permits and licensed work

In North Carolina, plumbing and gas work is licensed work. You can check a contractor on the public license search of the State Board of Examiners of Plumbing, Heating and Fire Sprinkler Contractors at public.nclicensing.org. The Town of Knightdale Development Services can tell you whether your job needs a permit, and plumbing inspections are done through Wake County Permits and Inspections.

When a tank is the better choice

Tankless is not always the answer.

  • The gas line cannot be upgraded without major work.
  • The panel cannot support an electric whole-house unit.
  • The household is small and a modern tank already meets its needs.
  • The budget favors the lowest up-front cost.

Heat pump tank heaters are another option for electric homes. See our water heater installation page to compare.

Sizing: flow rate and temperature rise

Tankless units are rated by how much water they can heat per minute at a given temperature rise. The rise is the gap between the water entering the house and the temperature you want at the tap. In winter the incoming water is colder, so the same unit delivers less hot water than in summer.

To size a unit, add up the flow rates of the fixtures that might run at once, such as two showers and a dishwasher, then check that the unit can deliver that flow at your winter rise. A plumber does this math in minutes. A unit that is too small is the number one reason people are disappointed with tankless.

What the first visit looks like

A good first visit is a measurement visit. We look at where the current heater sits, trace the gas line or check the panel, find the nearest path for venting and check whether a condensate drain or an outlet is within reach. We also ask about household size and any big water users, such as a soaking tub.

Then we give you a written price that lists the unit, the gas line or electrical work, the venting and the haul-away. If the house cannot support tankless without major work, we say so and show you the tank or heat pump alternatives, with no pressure to choose the more expensive path.

What drives the price

Every job is different, but these items move the number most.

  • Unit size and brand, and whether it is a condensing model.
  • Gas line upgrade, or a new electrical circuit and panel capacity.
  • Venting length and the route through the house.
  • Condensate drain and any new wall space needed.
  • Permits, where they apply, and removal of the old tank.

When to call a plumber right away

Call right away if you smell gas, see scorch marks near a vent, or find water pooling near the unit. If the gas smell is strong, leave the house and call from outside. Do not relight or open up a unit that has shown these signs.

Replacing a tank with an on-demand unit changes the gas line, the vent and sometimes the electrical panel, so this is not a weekend job for most homeowners. Ask for the work to be permitted where required.

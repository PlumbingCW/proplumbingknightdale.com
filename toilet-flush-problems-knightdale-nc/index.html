Toilet Flush Problems: A Knightdale, NC Homeowner Guide

.

A toilet that flushes weakly, runs on, or needs two tries usually has one of four problems: a worn flapper, a poorly set chain, clogged rim jets or a partial clog in the drain. Each has a different fix, and most of them take less than half an hour.

This guide shows how to tell them apart and when the cause is bigger than the toilet.

How a toilet flush works

Pressing the handle lifts a chain connected to a rubber flapper at the bottom of the tank. Water rushes into the bowl through the rim jets and a larger siphon jet, and a siphon pulls the contents into the drain. The flapper then drops and the fill valve refills the tank.

Any weak link in that chain shows up as a weak or incomplete flush: a flapper that closes too early, a chain that is too loose, jets that have clogged or a drain that cannot take the water.

Diagnose by symptom

Start with what you see and hear.

Safe fixes to try

Each of these is a homeowner job. Shut off the supply valve behind the toilet before opening the tank.

  • Check the chain. It should have about half an inch of slack when the flapper is closed.
  • Clean the rim jets with a small mirror and wire, or pour a cup of vinegar into the overflow tube and let it sit.
  • Lift the flapper and wipe the seat. If the rubber is soft or warped, replace it.
  • Adjust the water level to the mark inside the tank.
  • Use a flange plunger, not a cup plunger, to clear a partial clog.

When the problem is bigger than the toilet

If you have a weak flush on every toilet in the house, or toilets and tubs gurgle together, the cause is likely in the main sewer line or the vent system, not in any one fixture. That kind of problem needs a cable or a camera.

Our sewer line cleaning page explains what to expect. If only one toilet misbehaves, the tank parts or a localized clog are more likely.

Knightdale note

Wipes and the Knightdale sewer

Raleigh Water, which serves Knightdale, lists grease and so-called flushable wipes among the main causes of sewer backups, and its guidance is to keep wipes out of the toilet. That applies in every Knightdale neighborhood, from the railroad-era houses near downtown to subdivisions such as Parkside and Planter's Walk south of US 64.

The practical point is that the toilet is not a trash can. If you keep wipes and paper towels out, a toilet that flushes weakly is more likely to have a simple part problem than a clog.

Fixture traps and venting are covered by the North Carolina State Building Code: Plumbing Code, the state's edition of the International Plumbing Code (IPC), which is why a gurgling toilet can point to a vent problem rather than a toilet problem.

Repair or replace the toilet

If the toilet has a cracked bowl or tank, leaks at the base or has flush problems that persist after new parts and a clear drain, replacement may be the better value. Older high-volume models can also use more water than newer ones.

We can reset or replace a toilet and check the flange at the same time. See toilet repair for what that involves.

Inside the tank: flapper, fill valve and chain

Lift the lid and look. The flapper is the rubber disc over the large opening at the bottom. The fill valve is the tall assembly that refills the tank, and the overflow tube is the vertical pipe that protects against spills. The chain connects the handle to the flapper.

A flapper wears out slowly. It softens, warps or collects mineral film, and then it stops sealing. A quick test is to drop food coloring into the tank and wait fifteen minutes. If color appears in the bowl without flushing, the flapper is leaking.

Water level and why it matters

Most tanks have a mark showing the correct water line, usually about an inch below the top of the overflow tube. If the level is too low, the flush has less force. If it is too high, water drains into the overflow tube and the fill valve runs constantly.

Most fill valves have an adjuster, either a screw or a float that slides on a rod. A small turn changes the level, so adjust in steps and flush to test.

Low-flow and dual flush toilets

Modern toilets use less water per flush than older ones, and their design depends on a well-shaped bowl and clean jets. A newer model can seem to flush weakly because the jets are clogged, not because the toilet is weak. Cleaning the rim jets often restores it.

Dual flush models have two buttons and a more complex valve. If one button works and the other does not, the valve or its linkage is the usual cause, and it is worth replacing the assembly instead of trying to rebuild it.

Why a toilet gurgles

A gurgle after flushing or when another fixture drains points to air moving through the system. A blocked vent or a partial main line clog can cause it. Because venting is on the roof and the main line is underground, you cannot fix either from the bathroom.

If a toilet gurgles and a nearby shower also drains slowly, treat it as a line problem and call a plumber before it turns into a backup.

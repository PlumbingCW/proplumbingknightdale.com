Tankless Water Heater Installation and Service in Knightdale, NC

.

Tankless water heaters heat water only as it flows, so they never run out in the middle of a long shower. They also have more moving parts and more sensitivity to scale than a tank, which is why installation details and a flushing schedule matter.

How a tankless unit works and where it can fail

When a hot tap opens, a flow sensor starts the burner or heating elements, and a heat exchanger warms the water on its way through. Because there is no stored supply, the unit has to deliver the full temperature rise at the flow rate you ask for.

That is why failures are usually about flow, ignition or scale. A clogged inlet filter, a lime-coated heat exchanger or a failed sensor can each leave you with lukewarm water or an error code, and the symptom looks the same to the person standing in the shower.

Flushing and descaling

Manufacturers recommend periodic flushing to keep the heat exchanger clear. Raleigh Water's November 2025 report shows total hardness of 23.9 mg/L as calcium carbonate at the plant tap, and Raleigh Water serves Knightdale. That is soft water, which slows scale. Soft water does not stop it completely, so follow the interval in your manual or ask us what fits your usage.

A flush uses a small pump, hoses and a descaling solution circulated through the unit's service ports. We also clean the inlet screen, which catches sediment before it reaches the heat exchanger.

Gas, electric and what your house needs

Gas tankless units need a gas line sized for the burner rating, plus a vent that matches the model. Electric tankless units need a large dedicated circuit, and a whole-house electric unit may need more panel capacity than an older home has.

Many houses in the Parkside and Planter's Walk areas south of US 64 were built in the era of tank heaters in a garage or closet. Swapping to tankless there is possible, but the gas line and venting are the first things we measure.

What decides the price of a tankless job

We quote a clear price before work starts. These variables move it most:

Fuel and unit rating.

A larger gas or electric unit costs more, as do whole-house models.

Gas line or circuit upgrades.

Undersized supply lines are the most common extra.

Venting.

Longer or reworked vent runs add labor and parts.

Service ports and isolation valves.

These make future flushing quick and cheap.

Condition of the old unit.

Removing a tank and patching its flue adds time.

Insurance note: tankless upgrades are usually elective. Ask your utility about rebates, and keep the model and serial number for any manufacturer warranty claim.

When to call us right away

Call at once if you smell gas, see scorching near the vent, or hear the unit rumble or pop. Leave the house and call from outside if the smell is strong. Do not relight or reset a unit that has shown these signs.

Water that is far hotter than the setting or a leak at the unit also warrants a call. Shut off the water to the unit and the power or gas if it is safe to reach the valves.

Permits and licensed work

Gas and plumbing work falls under North Carolina licensing. You can check any plumbing contractor at public.nclicensing.org, which is the public search of the State Board of Examiners of Plumbing, Heating and Fire Sprinkler Contractors. The Town of Knightdale Development Services can tell you whether your job needs a permit, and Wake County Permits and Inspections handles plumbing inspections.

If a unit is leaking or has stopped heating entirely, emergency water heater repair is the faster route.

Gas piping, venting and the relief setup follow the North Carolina State Building Code: Plumbing Code, the state's edition of the International Plumbing Code (IPC). An electric unit's circuit follows the National Electrical Code (NEC), which is why panel capacity is the first thing we check.

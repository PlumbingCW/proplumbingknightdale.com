How to Reset a Tankless Water Heater in Knightdale, NC

.

To reset a tankless water heater, turn off its power, wait a minute, turn it back on and press the reset button if your model has one. Many units also need a fresh start after a power flicker or a gas supply interruption. If the error comes back, the unit is telling you something is actually wrong.

Always check the manual for your brand, because error codes and reset steps differ. This guide covers the common sequence and the checks that fix most problems before you call for service.

A general reset sequence

These steps fit many gas and electric models. Your manual always comes first.

  • Turn off all hot water taps in the house.
  • Switch the unit off at its power button or unplug it. For an electric unit, flip its breaker.
  • Wait about a minute so the control board clears.
  • Power the unit back on and open a hot tap with steady flow.
  • Watch the display for an error code and note it.

If the unit has a physical reset button, press it once after the wait. Do not hold it or press it repeatedly.

Safety first: when not to reset

If you smell gas, do not reset or restart anything. Leave the house and call the gas utility or 911. If you see water leaking from the unit, shut off the water supply and power, and call a plumber.

A unit that repeatedly shuts down on an overheat or ignition error should not be reset over and over. Repeated resets can mask a developing problem with venting, flow or ignition.

The inlet screen and flushing

Many tankless problems trace back to the inlet water filter, a small screen at the cold water inlet that catches sediment. If it clogs, flow drops and the unit may not fire. Cleaning it is a simple task described in most manuals, and it requires shutting the water and gas off first.

The other common cause is scale on the heat exchanger. A flush with a small pump and descaling solution through service ports removes it. If your unit has no service ports, a plumber can add isolation valves so future flushes are easy.

When to call a plumber

Call for service if an error code returns after a reset, if the burner will not ignite, if you see leaks or if you smell gas or exhaust. Tell us the model number and the code, and we can usually tell you what is likely wrong before we arrive.

For a unit that has stopped heating entirely, water heater repair is the faster route. If the unit is beyond saving, we can help you plan a replacement.

Check gas and venting before you blame the heater

A unit that lights, runs a few seconds and shuts off is often starved of gas or air. Confirm that the gas valve to the unit is open and that other gas appliances are working. If the range and furnace run but the heater does not, the problem is more likely in the unit or its gas line.

Look outside at the vent termination. Leaves, bird nests and snow can block a vent, and a blocked vent trips the safety shutdown. Do not climb onto a roof to clear it. Call us if the vent is out of reach.

What to write down before you call

A few facts speed up a diagnosis and often let us bring the right part.

  • The brand and model number from the label on the unit.
  • The exact error code and what the unit was doing when it appeared.
  • Whether the problem happens with every tap or only some.
  • Whether it is gas or electric and how old the unit is.
  • Any recent work on the house, the gas line or the electrical panel.

Power supply problems that look like heater problems

A tankless unit depends on steady power for its control board, fan and igniter, even when it burns gas. A tripped outlet, a loose plug or a surge can trigger an error that clears with a proper restart. If the unit loses power repeatedly, check the outlet or breaker before assuming the heater is failing.

In older Knightdale houses, a shared garage circuit can trip when a tool or freezer starts. A dedicated circuit for the heater is a small electrical job that removes the cause.

A simple maintenance routine

Once a year, clean the inlet screen and flush the heat exchanger through its service ports, and have the venting checked. Between visits, listen for new noises and watch for temperature swings, which are early signs of scale or a failing sensor.

Keep the area around the unit clear of boxes and stored items. A unit that gets enough combustion air and has room to be serviced is a unit that lasts.

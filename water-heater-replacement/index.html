When a failed water heater is an emergency

A tank that is actively leaking can release a lot of water in a short time, and a gas unit that smells like gas is a safety issue before it is a plumbing issue. For gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 first. For a water leak, here is what to do while you wait for us:

  1. Turn the cold water supply valve on top of the heater a quarter turn clockwise until it stops.
  2. Switch off the heater. For electric units, turn off the dedicated breaker. For gas units, turn the gas control to off or pilot.
  3. Open one hot tap in the house to relieve pressure, and keep the family away from any wet floor near electrical outlets.
  4. Move stored items out of the water's path and photograph the leak and the nameplate for your records.
  5. Call (919) 944-7281. We answer 24/7 and can talk through a replacement plan when you call.

Replace or repair: how we decide

A leak from the tank body means corrosion has gone through the steel and no repair will hold. A leaking fitting, a failed element, a bad thermostat or a worn pressure relief valve is often fixable. Age matters too, because a fix on a worn-out tank buys little time.

Many homes in Parkside, Planter's Walk and the Mingo Creek subdivision date from the years after the late-1980s Mingo Creek sewer outfall opened land south of US 64. A heater that has never been replaced in a house of that age has far outlived the usual service life of a tank unit. If yours is that old, replacement is normally the better value than another repair.

What the U.S. Department of Energy says about heat pump water heaters

Heat pump water heaters are worth discussing if your house is electric. The Department of Energy describes them as two to three times more energy efficient than conventional electric resistance water heaters.

  • The unit needs at least 1,000 cubic feet of air space around it.
  • The location should stay between 40 and 90 degrees Fahrenheit year round.
  • They cool the room they sit in, so a cold or tight closet is a poor spot.
  • DOE advises hiring a qualified contractor, getting written estimates and confirming the company handles local permits.

That list is why we walk the install location with you before we quote a heat pump.

What changes the price of a replacement

We give you a clear price before work starts. These are the pieces that make one job cost more than another.

Unit type and size.

A tankless or heat pump unit costs more up front than a like-for-like tank.

Venting and fuel connections.

Moving from a tank to tankless can mean a bigger gas line or new venting.

Code items.

Expansion tank, pan, relief valve discharge and shut-offs may need updating.

Removal and haul-away.

The old tank comes out with us, and access affects the labor.

Emergency timing.

Nights and holidays are priced differently than a scheduled install.

Insurance note: a tank that fails from age is usually a maintenance item, not a covered loss. Water damage from a sudden leak may be covered, so photograph everything and ask your insurer before you throw anything away.

Permits, licensing and who should do the work

Water heater replacement is licensed plumbing work in North Carolina. The State Board of Examiners of Plumbing, Heating and Fire Sprinkler Contractors licenses plumbing contractors, and you can check any contractor on its public license search at public.nclicensing.org.

Permit and inspection rules vary by project, so ask the Town of Knightdale Development Services whether your job needs a permit. Inspections for plumbing work in Knightdale are handled by Wake County Permits and Inspections, and permits go through the county's online portal. Soft water and your new heater

Raleigh Water's November 2025 finished water report lists total hardness of 23.9 mg/L as calcium carbonate at the plant tap, and Raleigh Water serves Knightdale. That is on the soft end of the usual scale, so scale buildup is slower here than in hard-water areas.

Soft water does not mean no maintenance. Sediment still collects and the anode rod still wears. We cover the schedule in our guide on tankless water heater flushing, and the same habits help tank units.

New installs are done to the North Carolina State Building Code: Plumbing Code, the state's edition of the International Plumbing Code (IPC), which sets out items such as the relief valve discharge line and shut-offs. Electric and heat pump units also need a circuit that follows the National Electrical Code (NEC).

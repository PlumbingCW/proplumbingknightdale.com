Commercial emergency: first steps

A plumbing failure in a business is also a safety and compliance matter. Do this first:

  1. Shut off the water at the nearest valve or the main.
  2. Stop kitchen or restroom operations that use the affected drains.
  3. Keep customers and staff away from any wet floor or sewage.
  4. Photograph damage for insurance and note the time it started.

Grease, floor drains and storm drains

Commercial kitchens produce a steady stream of grease. A grease trap catches much of it, but the lines still coat over time. We clear those lines and recommend a jetting interval that fits how heavy the kitchen is.

Storm and floor drains in parking lots and back-of-house areas collect leaves, grit and trash. We clear them and check where they discharge so a heavy rain does not turn into a flooded loading dock.

Backflow and cross-connection protection

Many commercial buildings need a backflow assembly that keeps non-potable water from flowing back into the public supply.

  • Assemblies typically need periodic testing by a certified tester.
  • A failed test means repair or replacement.
  • Your water utility sets the rules for your property, so ask it which devices and tests apply.
  • We repair and replace assemblies and coordinate testing.

Raleigh Water provides water service to Knightdale, so contact it about the specific cross-connection requirements for your building.

Permits and licensing

Commercial plumbing is licensed work in North Carolina. You can verify a contractor at public.nclicensing.org, the public search of the State Board of Examiners of Plumbing, Heating and Fire Sprinkler Contractors. The Town of Knightdale Development Services can tell you whether your project needs a permit, and plumbing inspections go through Wake County Permits and Inspections.

Backflow protection and grease interceptors for businesses fall under the North Carolina State Building Code: Plumbing Code, the state's edition of the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and your water utility sets the testing rules for your property.

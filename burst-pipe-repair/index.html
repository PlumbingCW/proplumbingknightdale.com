What to do in the first ten minutes

Speed matters, but so does safety. Follow this order:

  1. Shut off the main water valve, usually near where the line enters the house, or at the meter if the house valve will not turn.
  2. Turn off electricity to wet areas if you can reach the panel safely. Do not touch outlets or switches standing in water.
  3. Open the lowest faucet and flush toilets once to drain remaining water from the lines.
  4. Move furniture and valuables away from the water and photograph damage for insurance.
  5. Call (919) 944-7281. We will guide you and send a plumber.

Why pipes burst

Knowing the cause helps decide whether this is a one-time repair or a pipe-condition problem.

Cause.Typical sign.Typical outcome.
Freezing.Burst after a hard cold snap in an exposed wall or crawlspace.Repair the section and insulate.
High water pressure.Repeated leaks at fittings.Check pressure reducing valve.
Corrosion or age.Pinhole leaks along old galvanized or copper.Repair now and consider repiping.
Physical damage.Break after a nail, saw or excavation.Section repair.
Poor joint or fitting.Leak at a joint soon after earlier work.Redo the joint.

Winter pipes in eastern Wake County

Knightdale is not a deep-freeze climate, so burst pipes tend to come after a hard night when pipes in crawlspaces, attics, exterior walls and garages go unprotected. Homes with a crawlspace or pipes along a north wall are the usual victims.

  • Know where your main shut-off is before winter.
  • Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls during a hard freeze.
  • Let a faucet drip when temperatures stay low overnight.
  • Disconnect and drain outdoor hoses, and insulate exposed lines.

The practical payoff: a thirty-second drip on a cold night costs far less than a flooded kitchen.

How we repair and what we recommend

We cut out the damaged section and replace it with new pipe and fittings that suit the existing material, whether copper, CPVC or PEX. After the repair, we restore pressure slowly and check nearby joints.

On older homes, repeated leaks along the same line mean the pipe is wearing out. We say so plainly and lay out your choices, including repiping, without pressure to decide that day.

What decides the cost of a burst pipe repair

We quote a clear price before work starts. These factors matter most:

Location of the break.

An exposed line costs less than one inside a finished wall.

Pipe material.

Copper, PEX and CPVC use different fittings and methods.

Extent of damage.

One section versus several runs changes the scope.

Access.

Drywall or ceiling openings need patching, usually by another trade.

Time of day.

Nights, weekends and holidays are priced differently.

Insurance note: sudden pipe bursts are commonly covered for the resulting water damage under homeowners policies, though the pipe itself may not be. Photograph everything, stop the water and call your insurer promptly. Ask about a mitigation company if the house is soaked.

Drying out and next steps

Plumbing stops the water. Drying the structure is a separate job, so do not wait on insurance paperwork before starting. Standing water in walls and subfloors can lead to mold, so contact a water mitigation company if more than a small area is wet.

If the water source was in the yard rather than the house, water line repair is the likely fix.

Replacement pipe and fittings must suit the North Carolina State Building Code: Plumbing Code, the state's edition of the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and we test the repaired line under pressure before we leave.

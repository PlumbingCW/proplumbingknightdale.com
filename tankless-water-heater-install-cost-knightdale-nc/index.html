Average Cost of a Tankless Water Heater Install in Knightdale, NC

.

The cost of a tankless water heater install depends far more on your house than on the unit itself. The unit, the gas line or electrical circuit, the venting, the condensate drain, permits and removal of the old tank all change the total, and no honest average describes every home.

We do not publish a flat price, because a number that ignores your gas line or panel is not a number you can plan around. This guide lists what drives the cost so you can compare quotes fairly.

Why a house, not a unit, sets the price

Two homes can buy the same unit and receive very different quotes. One has a gas line that is already the right size and a clear wall for venting. The other needs a larger line, a new vent through the roof and a new condensate drain.

That is why a free phone quote for a tankless install is a guess. A visit is the only way to measure the gas line, check the panel and plan the vent. We recommend getting at least two written quotes and comparing the items in the table above.

Ways quotes can differ without being wrong

Different prices can be fair if the scope differs.

  • One quote may include a gas line upgrade and another may not.
  • One may include isolation valves and the other leaves them out.
  • One unit may be condensing and need a drain, and another may not.
  • One quote may include permit fees and inspection.
  • One may remove and haul away the old tank and another may not.
Knightdale note

Rebates, permits and Knightdale's soft water

Check with your electric or gas provider before you buy, because utilities and some programs offer rebates on high-efficiency water heaters. Rebate rules change, so ask for the current terms.

The Town of Knightdale Development Services can tell you whether your job needs a permit, and Wake County Permits and Inspections handles plumbing inspections. In North Carolina, you can verify a plumbing contractor at public.nclicensing.org. Raleigh Water's November 2025 report shows total hardness of 23.9 mg/L as calcium carbonate at the plant tap, which is soft, so scale is slower here.

A compliant install follows the North Carolina State Building Code: Plumbing Code, the state's edition of the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and electric units follow the National Electrical Code (NEC), which is why venting, gas and panel work show up as line items.

Sources: Town of Knightdale Development Services, Wake County Permits and Inspections, NC licensing board, Raleigh Water report, November 2025.

Running costs and the long view

A tankless unit avoids the standby heat loss of a tank, and many homeowners value never running out of hot water. The payback depends on your usage, your gas or electric rates and how long you stay in the house. A small household that uses little hot water gains less than a large one.

Maintenance matters too. A unit that is flushed on schedule and has isolation valves will last longer and cost less to keep running. Our tankless water heater page explains the routine.

Tankless versus tank versus heat pump

If the numbers do not favor tankless in your house, a standard tank or a heat pump water heater may be the better value. A tank has the lowest up-front cost, and a heat pump unit can have the lowest running cost in an electric home.

Our water heater installation page compares them, and we will tell you honestly when tankless is not the right answer.

How to compare quotes

A simple checklist.

  • Ask each contractor to list unit, gas or electrical work, venting, drain, permit and haul-away.
  • Check each contractor's license at public.nclicensing.org.
  • Ask what the labor and parts warranty covers.
  • Ask what happens if they find a problem once the old unit is removed.
  • Choose the clearest and most complete quote, not the shortest.

Red flags in a tankless quote

Be careful with any quote that gives a price without looking at the house, does not mention gas line size or panel capacity, leaves out venting or says nothing about the permit. Also be careful if the quote pushes a single brand with no explanation of sizing.

A solid quote names the model, the flow it delivers at your winter temperature rise, the gas or electrical work, the venting, the drain, the permit and the warranty. If one of those is missing, ask.

Timing your install

The cheapest time to replace a water heater is before it fails. A planned install lets you compare quotes, choose a unit and schedule the work. An emergency install at night limits your choices and is priced for the timing.

If your tank is over ten years old, start collecting quotes now. See water heater replacement for what to do if the unit has already failed.

Financing, warranties and what to expect afterward

Ask whether the manufacturer's warranty covers the heat exchanger for a longer period than other parts, and whether it requires proof of annual maintenance. Some warranties are voided by missing service ports or an unlicensed install.

After the install, keep the receipt, the model and serial number and the permit paperwork in one place. That folder helps with warranty claims and with a future sale of the house.

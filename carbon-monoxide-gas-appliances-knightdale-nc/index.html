Carbon Monoxide Leak Detection for Knightdale, NC Homes

.

Carbon monoxide is a colorless, odorless gas that fuel-burning appliances can produce when they are badly vented or damaged. You cannot see or smell it, so the only reliable defense is a working carbon monoxide alarm on every level of the home and outside each sleeping area.

This guide explains where CO can come from in a home with gas appliances, which warning signs matter, what to do if an alarm sounds and how a plumber fits into the picture.

If your alarm is sounding right now

Do not stop to investigate. Treat it as real.

  • Get everyone, including pets, outside into fresh air.
  • Call 911 or your fire department from outside.
  • Do not re-enter until emergency responders say it is safe.
  • Do not try to fix a gas water heater or furnace yourself afterward.
  • Have a qualified professional inspect the appliance before it is used again.

Symptoms such as headache, dizziness and nausea can come from CO. If anyone in the home feels them, get to fresh air and call 911.

Where carbon monoxide can come from in a home

Any appliance that burns fuel can make CO. In a typical Knightdale home that includes a gas water heater, a gas furnace, a gas range or dryer, a fireplace and a garage where a car is running. Most of the time the exhaust leaves safely through a vent. Trouble starts when the vent is blocked, cracked, disconnected or poorly sized.

Gas water heaters deserve particular attention because they sit in closets and garages where people rarely look. A flue that has pulled loose, a crushed vent pipe or a heater with a failing burner can all send exhaust into the room.

Alarm placement and upkeep

The Consumer Product Safety Commission advises installing battery-operated CO alarms, or alarms with battery backup, on every level of the home and outside sleeping areas. It notes that interconnected alarms are best, because when one sounds they all sound.

Test alarms regularly, replace batteries on a schedule you remember and replace the whole unit when its manufacturer says to. Most alarms have an end-of-life date printed on them. A silent alarm that has expired protects no one.

Knightdale note

Who inspects what in Knightdale

In North Carolina, gas piping and appliance connections are licensed work. Any contractor you hire to work on a gas water heater should hold the proper license, and you can look one up on the public license search of the State Board of Examiners of Plumbing, Heating and Fire Sprinkler Contractors at public.nclicensing.org.

Ask the Town of Knightdale Development Services whether a replacement or a change to venting needs a permit at your address. Plumbing inspections are handled through Wake County Permits and Inspections, and an inspected install is the best protection that the vent and combustion air were done right.

Venting of gas water heaters follows the North Carolina State Building Code: Plumbing Code, the state's edition of the International Plumbing Code (IPC) and the manufacturer's instructions, and a licensed plumber should do any change.

Sources: CPSC Carbon Monoxide Information Center, NC licensing board public search, Wake County Permits and Inspections.

What a plumber does and does not do

A plumber can check and repair the gas water heater, its vent pipe, its combustion air and its gas connections. We do not replace your alarms, and we do not diagnose a furnace or a fireplace flue. If an alarm has sounded and the heater is the suspect, we inspect it. If it is a furnace, you need an HVAC contractor.

When we replace a water heater, we install venting to the manufacturer's instructions and check the draft before we leave. Our water heater repair and water heater installation pages describe those steps.

A simple yearly safety routine

It takes less than an hour.

  • Test every alarm and replace batteries.
  • Look at the water heater and furnace flues for soot, rust or loose joints.
  • Keep the area around gas appliances clear of storage.
  • Never run a car in an attached garage, even with the door open.
  • Never run a portable generator inside the home or garage.

Why older Knightdale homes need a closer look

Houses near the railroad-era downtown have often had several heaters and furnaces over the decades, and each change may have altered the venting. Newer homes in subdivisions south of US 64 are tighter, which helps energy bills and makes proper combustion air even more important.

In either case, the best time to check venting is whenever any gas appliance is replaced.

Why combustion air matters

A gas burner needs a steady supply of air to burn cleanly. A water heater in a small closet with a solid door and no vents can starve for air, and a starved burner makes more carbon monoxide. Rules for how much open space or how many vents an appliance needs depend on its size and the manufacturer's instructions.

If you have recently sealed up a house with new windows or heavy weatherstripping, the air supply for a gas appliance can change. Ask a professional to recheck clearances and combustion air after major efficiency upgrades.

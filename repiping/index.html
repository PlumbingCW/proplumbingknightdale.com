.

Repiping replaces old or failing water lines with new pipe. It is a bigger job than a repair, so we only recommend it when the pattern of failures says repairs have stopped making sense. Knightdale Pro Plumbing repipes whole homes and partial runs in Knightdale, NC.

Signs it is time to repipe

One leak is a repair. A pattern is a decision. Look for these signs together:

  • Repeated pinhole leaks in different places along the same line.
  • Brown, red or rusty water when you first run a tap.
  • Low water pressure throughout the house that cleaning does not fix.
  • Visible corrosion, flaking or green crust on old pipe.
  • Pipe materials known for failure, such as polybutylene or aging galvanized steel.

Pipe materials and what they mean

The material in your walls decides how long it will last and what replaces it.

Material.Where you tend to see it.Our usual advice.
Galvanized steel.Older homes, often built before the 1960s.Replace when corrosion or low pressure appear.
Polybutylene.Homes built from the late 1970s into the mid-1990s.Plan replacement, since it is known for failing.
Copper.Many homes of all ages.Repair or replace based on condition.
CPVC.Newer homes and additions.Check for brittleness at older joints.
PEX.Newer homes and repipes.A common modern replacement.

What repiping involves

A repipe often means small openings in drywall at key points, new lines run through the walls, ceilings or attic and crawlspace, and new connections at fixtures and the water heater. We plan the route to minimize damage and explain which areas need access.

Water is off for part of the day, so we plan around your schedule. After the job, we test for leaks and pressure before anything is closed.

Which Knightdale homes are most likely to need it

Knightdale's older homes near the historic downtown, many built when the railroad shaped the town, are the likeliest to have galvanized or early copper lines. Subdivisions from the late-1980s Mingo Creek sewer outfall growth period, such as Parkside and Planter's Walk, are more likely to have copper, CPVC or, for some builders of that era, other materials worth checking.

That is why we start with an inspection of what you actually have. We then tell you whether repair, partial repipe or a full repipe is the sensible plan.

What decides the cost of a repipe

We quote a clear price before work starts. These are the main variables:

Size of the house.

More fixtures and longer runs mean more pipe and labor.

Pipe material.

PEX and copper differ in material and labor cost.

Access.

Slab homes, finished ceilings and tight crawlspaces add time.

Wall and ceiling repair.

Drywall patching is often a separate trade.

Scope.

A partial repipe of one problem line costs far less than a whole house.

Insurance note: repiping due to age or corrosion is normally not covered. A sudden failure that causes water damage may be, so photograph everything and ask your insurer.

What to do before we arrive

Find the main shut-off valve and make sure it turns. If it does not close fully, tell us when you call, because a repipe needs the water off reliably.

Plan for a day or more without running water in some rooms. Move valuables and clear the walls near the pipes, and tell us about pets or anyone with medical needs so the plan fits your household.

Water quality and your pipes

Raleigh Water's November 2025 finished water report shows total hardness of 23.9 mg/L as calcium carbonate at the plant tap. Soft water is not the same as corrosive water, so pipe condition still depends on material and age. If your water looks discolored, we check the pipe before assuming the source.

Permits and licensing apply. Check any contractor at public.nclicensing.org, and ask the Town of Knightdale Development Services about permit needs for your address.

Replacement piping, fittings and testing must meet the North Carolina State Building Code: Plumbing Code, the state's edition of the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and a repipe is the kind of job where a permit and inspection commonly apply.

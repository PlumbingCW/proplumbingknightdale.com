Under-Sink Plumbing Parts for Knightdale, NC Homes

.

Under almost every sink you will find the same five parts: a P-trap, a drain tailpiece, two supply lines, two shut-off valves and, in a kitchen, a disposal or dishwasher connection. Knowing which is which makes it easier to spot a leak early and talk to a plumber with the right words.

This guide names each part, shows what fails and tells you which fixes are safe to do yourself.

The parts and what they do

Open the cabinet and look for these.

Part.What it does.What fails.
P-trap.The curved pipe that holds water to block sewer gas.Loose slip nuts, cracked plastic, buildup.
Tailpiece and drain arm.Connect the sink drain to the trap and wall.Loose joints and worn washers.
Supply lines.Flexible lines carrying hot and cold water to the faucet.Corrosion, kinks and burst braided lines.
Angle stop valves.Shut off water to the faucet.Seized valves and slow drips at the stem.
Faucet shank and nuts.Hold the faucet to the sink.Loosening and leaks below the sink.
Disposal and dishwasher connections.Link appliances to the drain.Loose clamps and cracked hoses.

How to inspect in five minutes

Put a dry paper towel at the base of the cabinet and under each joint, then run the water for a minute. A drip or a damp spot shows up quickly. Feel the supply lines and valves for moisture and look for green or white crust, which is a sign of a slow leak.

Also look at the cabinet floor. Soft, swollen or stained wood means water has been collecting for some time. A small leak that is ignored for months can rot a cabinet and spread to the subfloor.

Safe homeowner fixes

Always close the shut-off valves or the main water first.

  • Tighten loose slip nuts on the trap by hand, then a quarter turn with pliers.
  • Replace a cracked plastic trap. It is inexpensive and takes minutes.
  • Replace braided supply lines that are kinked, corroded or older than about ten years.
  • Replace a leaking washer in the trap joints.
  • Clean out the trap with a bucket underneath to catch the water.

If a shut-off valve will not close or leaks when you turn it, stop. Forcing it can break the valve and flood the cabinet.

Why shut-off valves matter more than people think

Angle stops sit unused for years and then fail when you need them. A valve that will not close turns a small repair into a whole-house shutdown. Replacing a seized valve before it fails is one of the cheapest bits of plumbing insurance you can buy.

Knightdale note

Who to call for a leak, and where the boundary is

Raleigh Water provides water service in Knightdale and handles the main and the meter. Anything inside the house, including everything under your sinks, is the property owner's responsibility. If water is leaking and you cannot close a valve, shut off the main at the house or at the meter and call a plumber.

The Town of Knightdale Development Services can tell you whether replacing fixtures or moving plumbing needs a permit, and plumbing inspections are done by Wake County Permits and Inspections. Simple swaps of faucets, traps and supply lines typically do not involve a permit, but ask before you change drains or move fixtures.

Traps, supplies and shut-offs under fixtures are covered by the North Carolina State Building Code: Plumbing Code, the state's edition of the International Plumbing Code (IPC), so a simple like-for-like swap stays within code.

Sources: Raleigh Water service information, the Town of Knightdale Development Services and Wake County Permits and Inspections.

When to call a plumber

Call if a valve will not close, if the leak is at the wall, if the drain keeps backing up after you have cleaned the trap or if there is mold or swollen wood. Those are signs the problem goes beyond the parts under the sink.

A slow sink that does not clear after the trap is cleaned points to the branch line. See drain cleaning for that, and leak detection if you suspect a hidden leak.

Kitchen and bathroom sinks are different

A bathroom sink usually has a simple trap, a pop-up drain and two supply lines. A kitchen sink adds a second bowl or a disposal, a dishwasher connection and sometimes an air gap. More parts means more joints and more chances for a slow leak.

Bathroom cabinets also hold cleaning products and toiletries, so a leak there often goes unnoticed. Check both rooms twice a year and after any work done on the plumbing.

Dishwasher and disposal connections

The dishwasher drain hose should rise high under the counter before it connects, which helps keep sink water out of the dishwasher. Loose clamps, cracked hoses and clogs in the disposal inlet are the usual causes of a leak at this connection.

If your dishwasher leaves standing water and the sink drains slowly, the branch line or the disposal inlet is likely partly clogged. Clearing that is a drain job, not a dishwasher repair.

What a leak under the sink costs if you wait

A drip of a few drops a minute adds up to gallons a week, and it soaks into the cabinet base, the flooring under it and the wall behind it. Particleboard cabinets swell and crumble when wet, and a damp cabinet is where mold likes to start.

Catching it early usually means a ten-dollar part and ten minutes. Catching it after the cabinet floor has softened means a carpentry repair on top of the plumbing.

Protect the cabinet

A few inexpensive habits prevent most water damage.

  • Keep a small tray or mat under the trap so a drip shows up early.
  • Do not store cleaners where they can be pushed against a valve or hose.
  • Replace braided supply lines at the first sign of corrosion.
  • Learn where the main shut-off is, so you can close it if a sink-level valve fails.
  • Photograph the plumbing after any repair, so you have a record.

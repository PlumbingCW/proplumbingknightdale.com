How to Shut Off Your Home's Water in Knightdale, NC

.

Your main water shut-off valve is usually where the water line enters the house, in a utility room, garage, crawlspace or basement. There is also a shut-off at the meter near the street. Turn the house valve clockwise until it stops. If it will not turn, use the valve at the meter or call a plumber.

Finding the valve before you need it takes ten minutes and can save thousands of dollars. This guide shows where to look, how to close it and what to do if it fails.

The short version

Do this today, not during a flood.

  • Walk the house and find the main valve. Tag it with tape.
  • Test it. Close it fully, open a tap to confirm the water stops, then open it again.
  • Find the meter and its shut-off near the street.
  • Write down where each is and tell everyone in the house.
  • Check the valves under sinks and behind toilets while you are at it.

Where main valves hide

The location depends on the foundation and the age of the house.

House type.Where to look.What it looks like.
Crawlspace home.Near where the water line enters, often by the front wall or beside the water heater.Round wheel handle or a lever.
Slab home.Utility closet, garage wall or beside the water heater.Lever or wheel handle.
Home with a basement.Near the front foundation wall at floor level.Wheel handle.
Any home.At the meter near the street, in a ground-level box.A valve under the meter lid.

How to close each type

A wheel handle, or gate valve, closes clockwise, and it can take several full turns. Close it fully, then give it a gentle extra snug. A lever handle, or ball valve, closes with a quarter turn. When the lever is across the pipe, the valve is closed. When it is along the pipe, it is open.

After closing, open the lowest faucet in the house to drain the lines and the highest faucet to let air in. If water keeps coming, the valve is not fully closed or has failed.

What if the valve will not turn

Old gate valves corrode and seize, and forcing one can snap the stem and make the problem worse. If a valve is stuck, do not use a wrench with a cheater bar. Go to the meter and use the utility's shut-off, which is the right tool for that job.

The meter valve is often turned with a special key or a wrench, and utilities may ask that only they operate it. In an emergency, you can shut it, and then call a plumber and the utility. A seized house valve should be replaced, and it is a fast job for us. See burst pipe repair for what happens next.

Knightdale note

Raleigh Water and your meter

Knightdale's water is provided by the City of Raleigh through Raleigh Water, which handles the main and the meter. The line from the meter to your house is generally the property owner's. If water is surfacing near the meter or in the street, call Raleigh Water. If the problem is on your property, call a plumber.

The Town of Knightdale Development Services can tell you whether replacing a main valve needs a permit, and Wake County Permits and Inspections handles plumbing inspections. Replacing a seized valve inside your house is usually a simple repair, but ask if you are unsure.

Main shut-off valves and their placement are covered by the North Carolina State Building Code: Plumbing Code, the state's edition of the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and a licensed plumber can replace a failing one to code.

Sources: Raleigh Water service information, Town of Knightdale Development Services.

After the water is off

Shutting the water stops the flood, not the damage. Turn off breakers for wet areas if you can reach the panel safely. Move belongings away from water and start removing standing water. Photograph everything for insurance.

Call a plumber to find the source of the leak, and call your insurer. If a pipe has burst, our burst pipe repair page explains how we work. If you cannot find the source, leak detection can.

Freeze nights and trips away

Two times when knowing the valve matters even more.

  • Before a long trip, close the main and open a tap to drain the lines, especially in winter.
  • On hard freeze nights, know how to shut off quickly if a pipe splits.
  • Insulate exposed pipes in crawlspaces, garages and exterior walls.
  • Consider a leak sensor near the water heater and under sinks.
  • Keep the area around the main valve clear.

Replace the old valve before it fails

A gate valve that has never been turned in twenty years is a failure waiting for a flood. Replacing it with a full-port ball valve takes a plumber about an hour, provided the main can be shut off at the meter while we work. The newer valve closes with a quarter turn and rarely seizes.

Plan the replacement for a daytime visit and tell us if you want other valves, such as the ones at the water heater and under the sinks, replaced at the same time. Doing them together saves a second shutdown.

Teach everyone in the house

A valve only helps if the person who is home knows where it is. Walk everyone in the household through it, including teenagers and house sitters. Put a note near the valve with the direction to turn and the plumber's number.

If you rent out part of a property, make sure tenants know too, and keep a spare key or wrench handy if the meter box needs one.

Do not forget the water heater

After you close the main, the water heater still holds a tank of hot water. If a pipe has burst, the heater may keep supplying it through the hot side until you shut it. For a leaking heater, close the cold supply valve on top of the tank and switch off its power or gas.

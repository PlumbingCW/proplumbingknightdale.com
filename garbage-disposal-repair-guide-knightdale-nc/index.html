Garbage Disposal Repair Guide for Knightdale, NC Homes

.

Most garbage disposal problems fall into four groups: a jam, a tripped reset, a leak or a clog in the drain behind it. A jam and a reset you can often fix yourself in ten minutes. A leak from the housing means replacement, and a clog behind the disposal is a drain problem rather than a disposal problem.

This guide walks through each case in the order you should check them, shows what is safe to try and tells you when it is time to call a plumber in Knightdale.

Start with safety

A disposal has a spinning flywheel and a powerful motor, so the first step is always to cut power. Unplug the unit under the sink, or switch off its breaker if it is hardwired. A wall switch alone is not enough, because someone can flip it while your hand is near the blades.

Never reach into the grinding chamber with your fingers, even with the power off. Use tongs, pliers or the handle of a wooden spoon. If you cannot see what is jammed with a flashlight, stop and call a plumber.

How to free a jam step by step

This takes about five minutes with a hex key, which often comes with the unit.

  • Unplug the disposal or turn off its breaker.
  • Shine a light down the drain and remove any visible object with tongs.
  • Insert the hex key into the socket in the center of the bottom of the unit and work it back and forth until it turns freely.
  • Press the red reset button on the bottom, restore power and run cold water.
  • Test with a little water and no food before you use it normally.

If it hums again right away, or the reset keeps tripping, the motor may be failing. That is a replacement, not a repair.

What you should never put in a disposal

A disposal chops food into smaller pieces, but it does not make every food safe for the drain. The worst offenders are grease, fats and oils, which cool in the pipe and coat the walls. Coffee grounds, eggshells and rice pack into a paste, and stringy or starchy scraps such as celery, corn husks and potato peels tangle and clump.

Cold water helps move waste through, and a few seconds of extra running after the grinding stops carries scraps past the trap. Hot water on grease simply moves the problem farther down the line before it cools.

Knightdale note

What Raleigh Water says about grease and disposals

Knightdale's water and sewer are provided by Raleigh Water, and the utility names grease and so-called flushable wipes as two of the main causes of sewer backups. Its guidance for homes is to wipe grease from pans with a paper towel and put the towel in the trash, because a garbage disposal is not designed to handle fats, oils and grease.

That matters in a town with older drain lines. Houses near the old downtown rail line have had decades of buildup in their kitchen branches, and a disposal sends more solids into those bends. If your sink backs up even though the disposal runs, the line behind it is the problem, and drain cleaning is the right call.

Disposal and dishwasher drain connections follow the North Carolina State Building Code: Plumbing Code, the state's edition of the International Plumbing Code (IPC), including the high loop on the dishwasher hose.

Repair or replace

A jam, a tripped reset and a loose flange seal are all cheap fixes. A leak from the body of the disposal, a seized motor or a unit that is more than ten years old and failing is usually a replacement, since a repair on those costs close to a new unit.

When you replace, match the mounting style to your sink, choose a horsepower that fits your use and have the dishwasher drain connection checked. Our garbage disposal repair page explains how we handle installs, and we can also check for a clog behind it at the same visit.

Habits that make a disposal last

Small routines add years to the unit and keep the drain clear.

  • Run cold water before, during and for 15 seconds after grinding.
  • Feed scraps in small amounts rather than a full load at once.
  • Keep grease, coffee grounds and fibrous scraps in the trash or compost.
  • Clean the splash guard monthly with a brush and dish soap.
  • Grind a few ice cubes now and then to scour the walls.

What changes when you replace a disposal

Replacing a disposal is a two-hour job for a plumber and a long afternoon for a first-timer. The old unit comes off its mounting ring, the drain and dishwasher hoses are disconnected and the new unit twists onto the same ring if the style matches. If the mounting is a different brand, the ring and the sink flange come out too.

Two details cause most callbacks. The dishwasher knockout plug inside the new disposal must be removed if a dishwasher drains into it, and the dishwasher hose should loop up high under the counter before it connects. Both are easy to miss and both lead to a backed-up dishwasher.

Questions to ask before you buy a new disposal

Price and horsepower matter less than fit.

  • Will it fit the space under your sink, especially with a trap and dishwasher hose?
  • Does it use the same mounting style, or will the sink flange also need replacing?
  • Is the motor sized for your household's use, not just the lowest price?
  • Does it come with a cord, or does your outlet need one added?
  • Is the warranty on parts only, or does it include in-home service?

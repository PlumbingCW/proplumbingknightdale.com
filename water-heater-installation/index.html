What a proper install includes

A rushed install leaves problems that show up months later: a relief valve that drains onto the floor, a flue that sags, a missing expansion tank on a closed system. We treat the checklist below as the minimum, whatever the brand.

  • Sizing to your household's real hot water use, not the last unit's label.
  • New shut-off valves, supply connectors and a temperature and pressure relief valve with a proper discharge line.
  • Venting and combustion air checked against the manufacturer's instructions on gas units.
  • A drain pan and drain line where a leak would reach finished space.
  • Expansion tank where the system is closed, and a thermostat setting explained to you.
  • Removal and haul-away of the old unit.

Choosing between tank, tankless and heat pump

Each type solves a different problem. The honest answer depends on your fuel, your space and how your household uses hot water.

Heat pump water heaters: what the Department of Energy advises

The Department of Energy describes heat pump water heaters as two to three times more energy efficient than conventional electric resistance models. They pull heat from the surrounding air, so placement decides how well they perform.

  • They need roughly 1,000 cubic feet of air space around the unit.
  • The room should stay between 40 and 90 degrees Fahrenheit year round.
  • They cool and dehumidify the room, which helps in a warm utility space and hurts in a small closet.
  • DOE recommends hiring a qualified contractor and getting written estimates.

Many Knightdale homes put the water heater in a garage or a small utility closet, so we measure the space before we recommend a hybrid.

Permits, licensing and inspections

Replacing or moving a water heater is regulated plumbing work in North Carolina. The State Board of Examiners of Plumbing, Heating and Fire Sprinkler Contractors licenses the trade, and you can verify any contractor on its public license search at public.nclicensing.org.

Whether your job needs a permit depends on its scope. The Town of Knightdale Development Services can confirm that for your address, and plumbing inspections in town are carried out through Wake County Permits and Inspections. If a permit is needed, we explain the steps before work starts.

What changes the price of an installation

We quote a clear price before work starts. Here is what separates a simple job from a larger one.

Unit type.

A heat pump or tankless unit costs more up front than a standard tank.

Fuel changes.

Switching between gas and electric means new lines, circuits or venting.

Location.

Attic, garage and closet installs differ in labor and clearances.

Code updates.

Expansion tanks, pans, discharge lines and shut-offs may need to be brought up to current practice.

Schedule.

Planned installs cost less to schedule than emergency night calls.

Soft water and your install

Raleigh Water's November 2025 finished water report shows total hardness of 23.9 mg/L as calcium carbonate at the plant tap, and Raleigh Water serves Knightdale. That is soft water, so scale builds slowly compared with hard-water towns. It does not remove the need for periodic flushing, but it is good news for tankless heaters, which are the most scale-sensitive type.

Older homes near downtown and the Parkside and Mingo Creek areas south of US 64 are often on their second or third heater, so ask us to check the old shut-off valves and supply lines while we are there. Failing valves are the most common surprise during a swap.

Installs follow the North Carolina State Building Code: Plumbing Code, the state's edition of the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and electric or heat pump circuits follow the National Electrical Code (NEC). That is why a permit and inspection can apply to a job that looks like a simple swap.

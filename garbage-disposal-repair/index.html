Disposal leaking or jammed

Keep your hands out of the unit and do this first:

  1. Switch off the disposal and unplug it, or turn off its breaker.
  2. Never put your hand in the chamber. Use a hex wrench at the bottom or a wooden spoon handle from above to free a jam.
  3. If water is leaking under the sink, close the supply valves and place a bucket under the leak.
  4. Press the red reset button on the bottom once after the unit cools.
  5. Call (919) 944-7281 if it still will not run or keeps leaking.

Repair or replace

A jam or reset is a quick fix. A leak from the housing or a failed motor usually means replacement, since the cost of a repair approaches the cost of a new unit. A leak at the sink flange can often be sealed again.

We install new disposals with proper mounting, a new gasket and a check of the dishwasher connection. We also test the sink drain afterward so you know the trap and line are clear.

What not to put in a disposal

A disposal chops food into smaller pieces. It does not make grease, fibrous or starchy waste safe to pass through a drain.

  • Grease, fat and oil, which cool and coat the pipe downstream.
  • Coffee grounds, eggshells and rice, which pack into a sludge.
  • Celery, corn husks and potato peels, which tangle or paste.
  • Bones, fruit pits and non-food items.

Raleigh Water, which serves Knightdale, says a disposal is not designed to handle fats, oils and grease. Wipe pans with a paper towel and bin the towel instead.

Disposals and older Knightdale homes

Older kitchens in Knightdale's railroad-era homes sometimes have small drain lines with several bends under the sink. A disposal sends more solids into those bends, so the drain behind it matters as much as the disposal itself.

Newer subdivisions such as Parkside and Planter's Walk have simpler plumbing, but grease still catches in any trap.

The disposal's drain connection and the dishwasher hose loop follow the North Carolina State Building Code: Plumbing Code, the state's edition of the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and we check both when we install or replace a unit.
